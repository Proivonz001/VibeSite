Some bugs you can describe. This one I could only photograph.

Three points on the first circuit where cars slowed almost to a stop, then accelerated again. My theory was braking after a long straight before a big corner. I sent the screenshots and asked to understand the reason before touching anything. The detail that mattered was in the images: in two of the three cases the car slowing down was the leader, with a clear track ahead. So it was not traffic. It was systemic.

## Measuring instead of guessing

The method that made the difference on this project is one I would now recommend to anyone: instrument first, hypothesise later. Claude wrote a probe that compared, point by point along the lap, the geometry drawn on screen with the segment class assigned by the simulation at that point. The output was damning:

```
seg 10 (C1, slow corner) → drawn on a stretch with radius 600–2400 m (a straight!)
seg 11 (STRAIGHT)        → drawn inside a real corner
```

The simulation classified track sections by nominal metres, from a table generated by the track converter, and the classes were out of step with the real curve. So cars braked for a hairpin that on screen was a straight. First fix: a length-aware corner classifier that uses the drawn radius. Better, but not gone.

The second cause was the kind of bug that makes you laugh once you find it. The pipeline merged consecutive stretches with the same class "as a ring", wrapping around the end of the array. But the first and last stretches of a lap are always straights, the grid straight, so they were merged across the boundary, and every segment table came out rotated by one position. Every corner was assigned to the previous section's geometry. The classifier had been right all along; it was being fed a table rotated by one. Fixing `build_runs` to anchor on the line ended the phantom braking. The commit message reads "Fix the real phantom-braking culprit: segment table rotation".

## Crab cars

In between, a smaller mystery. Cars often ran tilted, crabbing along the track instead of pointing where they were going. I asked for the reason and a proposal without applying it, which is a habit I picked up on this project: let the diagnosis land first, then decide. The cause was the heading being computed from two points sampled on different lateral bases, so a car on the outside lane got the tangent of a different curve. Heading from the racing line's own tangent, sampled on a longer base of about seven metres, put every car straight, and side-by-side pairs finally ran parallel.

That night was also the night of the internet drops. Four times in a row my messages read "continue, I've restored the internet". The work resumed each time from the exact line it had stopped at, because everything was in the repository and in the project notes. That, more than any feature, is what made these long sessions possible.

## From classes to a speed profile

On 10 August I opened a session with a single objective: solve all the simulation and physics problems, at the root, so they cannot come back anywhere. Two more slow points had appeared, this time on the two segments of the track classified as hairpins at a fixed 94 km/h. The GeoJSON said otherwise: they were fast esses.

The conclusion was that classes should stop deciding speeds at all. A new module builds a **speed profile** from the racing line itself: curvature along the line gives a maximum cornering speed, then a forward pass for acceleration and a backward pass for braking produce a continuous, physically plausible velocity along the whole lap. Segments still exist for strategy and gaps, but the speed a car carries at any point comes from geometry. The two esses went from 94 km/h to what the corner allows.

The same session added the ability to choose which of my two drivers the camera follows, and more zoom-out before the driver view hands over to the map. Small things, but they were on my list, and the list is the plan.
